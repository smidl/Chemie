2026-07-30 — **ROOT CAUSE CONFIRMED AT DFT: a collapsed multi-fragment embedding.** Repeated the test at ab-initio level on `spec01_cyanohydrin` (the specialty set's own positive control) with hand-written H-explicit atom-mapped SMILES. Arm D reproduced `NON_MONOTONIC_PATH`, and the endpoint energies exposed why: `e_react` = −209.75 Ha vs `e_prod` = −284.66 Ha — a **75 Hartree gap between two structures with identical atoms**, impossible as chemistry. The geometry the pipeline was handed has a **minimum interatomic distance of 0.142 Å** (acetone carbonyl C on top of the HCN carbon; a C–C bond is 1.54 Å), whole-system extent 4.18 Å. **RDKit's ETKDG has no intermolecular term for disconnected fragments, so embedding a multi-fragment reactant SMILES collapses the fragments into each other.** The pipeline then uses that geometry as an unoptimised single point for `e_react`, so the endpoint sits tens of Hartree too high; the NEB relaxes the interior images to sane structures which are consequently far BELOW the endpoint, and the criterion fires. There IS an interior maximum — it is just below a garbage endpoint. Explains 3/3 bimolecular failures and 450/450 on Transition1x simultaneously. **FIX (revised, bigger than "optimise the endpoints"):** embed each fragment separately → place as a pre-reaction complex (reacting atoms 2.5–3.5 Å) → optimise the complex at the NEB's level → and add a **pre-flight guard**: reject endpoints with min interatomic distance < ~0.8 Å and check endpoint energy against the sum of separately-optimised fragment energies. That guard is the highest value-per-line change in the whole thread: milliseconds versus the 81 minutes of DFT that were spent on physically invalid inputs.
