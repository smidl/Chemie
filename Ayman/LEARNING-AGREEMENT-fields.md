## Detailed programme of the traineeship period

The trainee will carry out a research study on the sensitivity of active learning to the noise
assumptions it relies on, using chemical reaction datasets as a testbed.

- **Months 1–2** — literature review; familiarisation with the existing codebase, the cluster
  environment and the datasets; reproduction of a published baseline.
- **Month 3** — correction of two known methodological defects in the existing implementation
  (batch selection, replication of the initial design); definition of the statistical protocol.
- **Month 4** — characterisation of the noise structure of real high-throughput reaction data from
  replicate measurements.
- **Months 4–5** — factorial simulation study measuring how active-learning performance responds to
  controlled changes in noise structure.
- **Months 5–6** — investigation of whether the effects found can be mitigated by changing the
  acquisition rule; writing up.

Work is on-site. Computation runs on the institutional cluster, accessed remotely from the host
institution; there is no separate virtual mobility component.

## Knowledge, skills and competences to be acquired

**Knowledge.** Theory and practice of active learning and sequential experimental design;
uncertainty quantification in machine learning, including the distinction between reducible and
irreducible uncertainty; the structure and limitations of experimental chemistry datasets.

**Skills.** Design and execution of controlled computational experiments; statistical evaluation
with appropriate replication and confidence intervals; use of high-performance computing with batch
scheduling; reproducible research practice using version control; scientific writing.

**Competences.** Ability to formulate a research question independently and to design experiments
that can falsify it; critical assessment of published claims, including recognising when a negative
result is due to implementation rather than to the phenomenon; ability to present and defend
methodological choices; independent time management across a multi-month project.

## Monitoring plan

- Weekly supervision meeting to review progress, results and next steps.
- All code, data-processing scripts and results kept under version control in a shared repository,
  visible to the supervisor continuously.
- A written progress log maintained by the trainee, updated at least fortnightly.
- A formal mid-term review at the end of month 3, at which the corrected baseline and the
  statistical protocol are assessed and the remaining plan is confirmed or adjusted.
- The receiving supervisor is available for ad-hoc consultation between scheduled meetings.

## Evaluation plan

Assessment is continuous, based on the following criteria:

- **Technical execution** — correctness and reproducibility of the experiments; quality of the code.
- **Scientific rigour** — appropriate experimental design, adequate replication, honest reporting of
  negative and inconclusive results.
- **Independence** — progressive reduction in the guidance required.
- **Communication** — clarity of the written progress log, the final report and the oral
  presentation.

Formal evaluation points are the mid-term review at month 3 and a final assessment at month 6,
comprising the written report and an oral presentation to the research group. The receiving
supervisor completes the traineeship certificate and the final evaluation form required by the
sending institution.
